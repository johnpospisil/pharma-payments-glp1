# Clinical review checklist

**What this is.** *Lunch, Speaking Fees and Semaglutide* is an independent data project that compares drug-maker payments to Medicare prescribers (CMS Open Payments) with the same prescribers' GLP-1 prescribing in Medicare Part D, 2013–2024. Before it is shared publicly, I would value a clinician's read of the parts that depend on clinical and market knowledge. The statistics have been reviewed separately.

**What I'm asking for.** About 45–60 minutes. Read the article, then go through the questions below. Short answers are fine: "fine", "wrong because…", or "you're missing…". Disagreement is the most useful thing you can give me.

**What you'll need.**
- The article: *(link: the article URL, or the PDF attached)*
- Optional, the explorer by specialty and state: *(link: the explorer URL)*
- No individual prescriber is named, mapped or ranked anywhere, and you will not be asked to judge any individual's prescribing.

## 1. The drugs and the market (most important)

1. **Brand choice.** The main finding is that after a company's first payment, its brand's share of the doctor's GLP-1 claims rises by a few percentage points, mostly at the expense of direct competitors. Which clinical reasons, independent of any payment, would make a doctor shift between Trulicity, Victoza, Ozempic and Mounjaro in the same years? Examples: weekly vs daily dosing, potency, cardiovascular evidence, formulary changes, patient requests.
2. **Timing of events.** Do any of these line up with the payment waves closely enough to explain a shift on their own? Is anything important missing?
   - The cardiovascular indications: Victoza 2017, Ozempic January 2020, Trulicity February 2020.
   - The 2022–2024 semaglutide and tirzepatide shortages.
   - Lilly moving promotion from Trulicity to Mounjaro.
   - Generic liraglutide in 2024.
3. **Formularies.** In your experience, how much does Part D formulary tier or prior authorisation decide which GLP-1 a Medicare patient gets, compared with prescriber preference?
4. **Victoza.** The paid brand's share *falls* after Victoza payments. Is the explanation plausible: Victoza payments came while daily Victoza was being displaced by weekly drugs, including Novo Nordisk's own Ozempic?
5. **Mounjaro.** Its whole observation window (2022–2024) falls in the shortage years. What should a reader keep in mind about early Mounjaro prescribing?

## 2. What the numbers measure

6. **"Claims".** The study counts Part D claims, meaning pharmacy fills attributed to the prescriber, not patients or new prescriptions. Does anything about GLP-1 dispensing distort claims-based comparisons between brands? Examples: dose-titration fills, 28-day pens, 90-day supplies.
7. **The 11-claim line.** Medicare hides any prescriber-drug row with fewer than 11 claims a year, so the study treats "11 or more claims of a brand in a year" as regular prescribing. Is that a fair description, and roughly how many patients does it imply?
8. **Statins as a check.** The study uses statin prescribing as a "negative control": a GLP-1 payment should not change it. Since GLP-1 promotion now emphasises cardiovascular risk, could a GLP-1 sales visit plausibly prompt statin prescribing too?
9. **Diabetes, not obesity.** The study treats Part D GLP-1 use as diabetes use, because Part D cannot cover weight-loss-only prescribing. Wegovy (covered for cardiovascular risk from March 2024) and Zepbound are excluded. Is that right? How much off-label Ozempic or Mounjaro use for weight would you expect to have reached Part D?

## 3. Who is studied

10. **Specialty groups:** endocrinology, primary care (MD/DO), cardiology, nephrology, other physicians, nurse practitioners, physician assistants, and others (largely pharmacists and residents). Do these make sense for GLP-1 prescribing? Should cardiology and nephrology be read differently, given their SGLT2 use for heart failure and kidney disease?
11. **Meals versus speakers.** Most newly paid doctors were first reached through a sponsored meal worth under $100. Speakers were mostly first paid before the study window. Does the article's description of a typical sponsored meal match your experience?

## 4. Fairness and tone

12. Is anything in the article unfair to physicians, NPs or PAs? Does any sentence or chart imply that prescribing more of a brand is inappropriate?
13. Is there a sentence you would expect a colleague to object to, and why?
14. Would you be comfortable seeing this shared in a clinical forum (for example r/medicine), as written?
