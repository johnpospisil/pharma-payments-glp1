## Methodology review: claims that are still weak

Overall assessment (validate-data scale): **share with noted caveats.** The two framings already decided (D31 scope, D34 lead with brand share) are the right ones. The caveats below must travel with the numbers into the article (P22), the app (P23) and the launch posts (P27).

| # | Claim | Why it is weak | How the article must state it |
|---|---|---|---|
| W1 | Paid physicians become more likely to prescribe ≥ 11 claims of the brand (+12.0 / +5.5 / +13.7 pp) | Unmatched pre-trends are steep and rejected. Statins rise almost as much (+6.4 / +7.9 / +7.1 pp), even after volume-growth matching (DEV-8). The pre-trend in other GLP-1 prescribing is rejected for all three brands without matching (with matching, only Ozempic passes). The Ozempic → Trulicity placebo brand fails in levels. §10 verdict: "explained at least partly by targeting". | As targeting of growing practices (D34), never as an effect of payments. Every sentence quoting it is paired with the statin result. |
| W2 | The paid brand gains 3–5 pp of the physician's GLP-1 share (H5) | Precise under the linear estimators. Under the registered log-odds form, per-brand CIs include 0 except Mounjaro, and the unmatched log-odds estimates are ≈ 0. The pooled H5 test (+3.2 pp, Holm p = 0.021) rests on matching. The matched Trulicity share pre-trend is rejected (p = 0.007), although it runs opposite in sign to the effect. | "Consistent with a brand-specific shift", with the registered-form imprecision in the same paragraph (D29). Victoza's share falls (−4.8 pp) and is reported alongside. |
| W3 | Negative-control exposure | Anticoagulant payments pass the fail rule, but are still associated with 37% of the GLP-1 binary effect. Inhaled-respiratory payments (68%) fail. | "Part of the association appears with payments for unrelated drugs too." |
| W4 | Fills rise +28.7% (H6) | The size depends on how suppressed cells are bounded: matched PPML gives +17% to +111% across the 0 / 5 / cap bounds. The unmatched Ozempic estimate at the 0 bound is −0.5%. | Quote only the conservative (cap) bound, with the range. |
| W5 | Gradient by first-year dollars and contacts (H4, +5.1 pp) | Higher bins are more selected. This is descriptive, not a dose-response. | "Physicians who received more were also more likely to…", with the selection caveat (DEV-7). |
| W6 | NPs/PAs +15.0 pp (H7) | No matched or falsification versions. The pre-period is short (payments observed from 2021, with 2021 as washout). Victoza has only 19 treated. | Secondary, with the same targeting caveat as W1. |
| W7 | South > other regions; rural > urban | Subgroup ATTs inherit W1. Regional differences may reflect practice growth, formularies or Medicare Advantage mix, not responsiveness. | Descriptive heterogeneity, never "Southern doctors respond more". |
| W8 | Speaker fees vs meals (H2) | Not supported (+5.6 pp, Holm p = 0.12). Only 71–109 fee recipients per brand, mostly first paid before 2015 and so outside the event study (D31). | State that the study cannot say whether speaker fees matter more. |
| W9 | State map (chart 6) | The 2024 snapshot attributes payments to the recipient's Open Payments state. Four states are masked by the dominance rule. The US figure includes them. | Note on the chart (done). Use no state rankings in social posts. |
| W10 | Registered / trajectory matching | Balanced on its own variables (max \|SMD\| ≤ 0.04), but not on pre-period total-claims growth (\|SMD\| 0.17–0.24). Only DEV-8 balances it, with near-identical estimates; statins still fail under DEV-8 | "Matched on prior prescribing"; never "matched on everything" |

**What does hold up:**
- The descriptive facts (spending, concentration, payments per diabetes workload).
- The direction of the share shift toward the paid brand and away from its direct competitors, under every linear estimator, matched or not.
- The placebo timing tests.
- The rebuild reproducing every published number (below).

## Disclosure

**Bugs found and fixed in P20:**
- **The P11 state CSV exposed small cells.** It printed Guam (4 paid prescribers) and the Virgin Islands (1), with dollars. Blanked.
- **Chart 6 needed the dominance rule.** Vermont's value was 92% one prescriber's dollars; Idaho 64%, Maine 55%, Oregon 53%. Masked, with a note on the chart.
- **National QA tables had cells of 1–10 prescribers.** Blanked at write time via `ppg.disclosure.mask_small` / `mask_crosstab`. These are national figures with no geography, but they go public with the repo.
- **The eda `usd_other` share dropped education dollars** (NaN + value). It was 0.02% and is correctly 0.15%. Not quoted anywhere.
- **Falsification labels:** the other-GLP-1 pre-trend rows were labelled as p-values but hold a pp ATT.
- **The README named Rybelsus as an analysed brand.**

**Determinism fixes (needed for item 4):**
- explicit tie-breaks for the practice (2,340 NPIs had tied practices), recipient class, OP specialty and profile NPI;
- dollars rounded to the cent, with half-cents ($99.995) rounded up consistently, before dose bins and in the brand-year table;
- sorted outputs (heatmap CSV, manufacturer jumps);
- SVGs written with a fixed hash salt and no date.

**Complementary disclosure:**
- The US total in chart 6 lets a reader derive the four masked states' combined dollars. That residual is spread across 1,580 prescribers, top share 22%, so it describes no individual.
- National brand-year totals minus shown natures can recover a masked nature subtotal for a national brand-year. It describes at most 10 unnamed prescribers nationally, with no geography or specialty, so it is not identifying. This is accepted and noted here.
