# Literature and prior art (P02)

Compiled 2026-10-22. Empirical findings are taken from each paper's own abstract (PubMed/NBER/AEA), not from press coverage. The *Mayo Clinic Proceedings* 2026 letter is the one exception: its full text is embargoed until January 2027, so only its design is known.

## What's new here (for the article)

Two decades of studies agree that physicians who receive drug-company payments prescribe more of the paying company's drugs. Almost all of them are cross-sectional snapshots that can't separate influence from targeting. The only GLP-1 study so far (Khunte et al. 2026) is also cross-sectional. This project follows **each prescriber, brand by brand, over 12 years (2013–2024)**, asks whether prescribing of a specific GLP-1 brand changes *after* the first payment for that brand compared with similar unpaid prescribers, and tests whether payments shift patients *between* competing brands. It uses estimators built for staggered timing, negative-control tests, and a public, reproducible pipeline.

## 4. Policy context the article must get right

- **Part D excludes drugs used only for weight loss** (statutory since 2006). Wegovy became coverable in March 2024 only for cardiovascular risk reduction in people with established heart disease and overweight/obesity. (CMS guidance as reported by KFF Health News/NPR, 2024-03-22; to be confirmed against CMS's own memo in P24.)
- **Medicare GLP-1 Bridge:** CMS programme starting **2026-07-01** with a $50/month copay for eligible beneficiaries, extended through 2027-12-31 ([cms.gov](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/coverage/prescription-drug-coverage/medicare-glp-1-bridge)). It falls after our outcome data end (2024), but readers will ask about it.
- Semaglutide was selected for the second round of Medicare drug price negotiation (prices effective 2027). Also after our data window; mention only as context.
- 2022–2024 shortages of semaglutide and tirzepatide (FDA shortage list) and compounding constrained supply. This is a **time-varying shock common to all prescribers of a brand** (absorbed by brand × year effects, but it can interact with targeting). P24 pharmacist review should confirm dates.

## 5. Design implications (proposed; each needs the user's OK before CLAUDE.md changes)

- **D-1 (from Carey et al.):** effects arrive within months, but our data are annual, so event year 0 is partly treated depending on payment month. Add a sensitivity check that moves the event year to t+1 when the first payment falls in the second half of the year, and report year 0 separately.
- **D-2 (from DeJong, Fleischman, Sharma):** manufacturer- and brand-specific exposures matter more than "any payment". Keep brand-specific exposure as primary, with manufacturer-level exposure (any Novo / any Lilly payment) as secondary.
- **D-3 (from Agha & Zeltzer, Hyman):** spillovers within practices bias effects toward zero and pollute controls. Get a **practice/group ID** (the public *Doctors and Clinicians* national file has group PAC IDs, which is also what Khunte et al. used for specialty) and (a) cluster standard errors by practice and (b) run a sensitivity check that uses only controls from practices with no paid member. This fits P05, where it also partly replaces the missing NPPES file.
- **D-4 (from Chen & Roth, Wooldridge):** make PPML/Poisson proportional ATTs the primary scale and log(1+y) a robustness check (decided formally in P12).
- **D-5 (from Fleischman):** pre-register a directional secondary hypothesis that speaker/consulting > meals, and specialists > primary care.
- **D-6 (from Khunte et al.):** Khunte et al. have published the cross-sectional association, so a cross-sectional result here is not news. The headline must be the within-prescriber, brand-level change and the switching result.

## What we must not claim

- That payments *cause* prescribing (at most: "consistent with influence after accounting for targeting and pre-trends").
- That extra GLP-1 prescribing is inappropriate. These drugs have strong evidence (Grennan et al.'s welfare point); we measure brand choice and volume, not appropriateness.
- Anything about obesity prescribing or the commercial market.
- Anything about an individual prescriber.
